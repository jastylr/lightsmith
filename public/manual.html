Lightsmith user manual

Lightsmith builds a light show for your band's songs and writes it into a QLC+ show file. You design each song in your browser; QLC+ runs the lights at the gig.

What Lightsmith does

QLC+ is free lighting software that drives your lights through a USB or Art-Net adapter. It can do almost anything, but building a show in it means making hundreds of scenes, chasers and buttons by hand. Lightsmith makes them for you from a few musical choices per song section: the mood, how much energy, which colors, what the moving heads do.

Lightsmith never controls the lights itself. It reads your QLC+ show file, adds the show to it, and gives you a new file to open in QLC+. Everything runs in your browser: your files and recordings stay on your computer.

It works with any lights QLC+ knows and any number of them. The examples here mention one band's rig (wash bars, LED panels and two moving heads), but nothing in Lightsmith is tied to it.

The five steps

The menu on the left walks you through them in order. You can go back to any step at any time.

Songs and sections

A song is a row of sections on a timeline: intro, verse, chorus and so on. Each block's width is its length, and its color is its look. Click a block to edit it; drag it to move it; drag its right edge to change its length.

The song's name at the top of the page is where you rename it: click it and type. The small arrow beside it switches to another song (the sidebar lists them too); + adds a song and the bin deletes this one. In a narrower window the transport moves to its own row under the name.

The transport

Beside the song's name sits the transport: Back to start, Previous section, Play and Next section, and a display of where you are, in bars, beats and sixteenths and in time, with the section playing and the song's length. Click the bar number, type a bar and press Return to jump there. Space plays and pauses; Return goes back to the start. The section buttons, and the ← and → keys, work like markers in a recording program: next jumps to the start of the next section; previous jumps to the start of this one, or to the one before if you're in its first second, so a double press steps back two. They work while playing, with the recording following along.

The song's tempo and time signature are on the display too: click either to type a new one (or use the arrow keys), or press Tap along with the beat. Sections that follow the song change with it; a section with a tempo or signature of its own keeps it, and the display says so while that section is playing.

Length, bars and snapping

Lengths are kept in bars at the song's tempo, so changing the tempo stretches every section with it. The Snap setting under the timeline decides how a dragged edge snaps: to whole Bars, to Beats, or Off for songs not played to a click. While dragging, hold Option/Alt to snap one step finer, or Option+Shift to not snap at all. You can also type an exact time, like 28s400ms, in the section's In time field.

Sections sit end to end, so a section's start is the end of the one before it: to move where a chorus starts, drag the right edge of the section before it.

Linked sections

A section can go straight into the next one. Then one cue plays both: the first runs for its length and the second starts by itself. Use it to give one part of a song two looks (the first half of a chorus calm, the second half big), or to move on when a sample of fixed length ends.

  • Split (the arrows icon on a section in the list) cuts a section into two linked halves with the same look. Change the second half's look to taste. Chasers and lightning stay with the first half, since they're timed from its start.
  • Hover the timeline and click the link badge where two blocks meet to link or unlink them. Linked pairs keep an orange badge.
  • The same setting is in the section's Timing card: "Wait for the next cue" or "Go straight into …".

You can chain more than two. The last section in a chain holds its look until the next cue.

Working with a recording

Add the song's recording (Add a recording under the timeline, or drop an audio file on it) to lay the sections out against the music. The recording stays in your browser; your project only notes which file belongs to the song, so on another computer Lightsmith asks you to load it again.

  • The waveform sits under the blocks, with lines for each bar, each section start and, zoomed in, each beat.
  • Starts at places the start of the recording on the timeline. 13s brings the music in 13 seconds into the song, after a sample you play live; a minus time such as -4s skips a count-in at the start of the file. You can also drag the waveform sideways, or move the playhead where the music should come in and click Use playhead.
  • Play on the transport (or Space) plays the music with the lights. Drag the playhead, or along the ruler, to move through the song.
  • Zoom with the buttons, or pinch / Cmd/Ctrl + scroll over the timeline. The tall-waveform button makes the waveform easier to read.

Designing a look

Click a section on the timeline to edit it. Its settings come in five steps, in the order a look is usually built: Look, Motion, Accents, Lights and Timing (or every card at once with All). A dot on a step means it has settings of its own. In a step with two cards (and in All), click a card's header to fold it down to a one-line summary; a folded card marked Changed has settings of its own. Folded cards stay folded as you move between sections, until you open them again. Start with the Look; most sections need little else.

On a wide screen the stage preview stays pinned beside the settings as you scroll, with a small transport under it (back to the start, previous section, play, next section, and a bar to move through the song), so you always see what you're changing. On a narrower screen it sits above the settings, and a small copy stays at the top once it scrolls away.

The section's header holds its name, its type (changing it sets a matching mood, energy and start), and buttons to split, duplicate or delete it. List shows the sections as a list beside the editor, for those who like one; the timeline has them all too, and hovering a block there shows its full name.

Shaping the song

A show lands when it rises and falls: calmer verses, a chorus that lifts, a peak, a clear ending. Three things help you shape it:

  • The energy line. Each block on the timeline has a dot on top, and a dashed line joins them, so you see the song's shape at a glance. Drag a dot up or down to set that section's energy (1 to 5); the block's height follows. With the dot focused, the up and down arrow keys do the same.
  • Show coach (under the stage preview) points out specific things worth a look: a chorus that's no bigger than the verse before it, or that looks just like it; a chorus that fades in slowly; three sections in a row that look the same; a whole song at one energy; moving heads that never move; a long section where nothing changes; and a song with no ending. Most come with a one-click fix (Undo takes it back) and a link to the section. Dismiss hides a suggestion for that song in this browser; a link brings dismissed ones back.
  • Variations (in the section's header) shows three other ways the section could look, each as a small moving preview beside yours: more motion (a moving pattern, and the heads moving), other colors, and a step bigger (or, at full energy, a step calmer). Use this makes it the section's look; Shuffle deals three more.

Copy this look to… (in the section's header) puts this section's whole look onto other sections of the song: tick them in the list (sections with the same name, like "Chorus copy", start ticked) and confirm. Their names, lengths, tempo and cues stay; Undo brings their old looks back. To use one look in several songs, save it as a preset instead.

The Show switch in the sidebar picks how much you see: Simple keeps to the essentials, Full shows every setting. It only hides controls, never changes the show; a hidden setting that's in use says so, with a link to show it. In Full, the moving heads' exact shape (width, height, rotation and so on) sits under Fine-tune the shape.

Fades between sections

With Fade in, every light glides from what's on stage to the new look over the length you pick; anything that's the same in both doesn't move. So to bring a spotlight up slowly, add a section with the same look plus Focus and give it a long fade. Patterns, chasers and moving-head movements start fresh with the section, and the heads move to a new position as fast as their motors allow. While a song plays, the preview fades the same way.

Looks you like can be saved as presets and reused in other sections and songs.

Moving heads

Position and movement

Position is where the heads point when they're still: their home. Movement is a shape they trace around that point (a sweep, a circle, a figure eight). So a "sweep over the audience" needs both: a home aimed over the crowd and a sweep movement. A movement around a home pointing straight up looks like a small, pointless circle.

Size and speed

Size is how far the movement reaches from home, each way. Speed is how many beats one round of the movement takes. A head can only turn so fast: in the Stage step, set How fast it moves for each head, and Lightsmith slows a movement down rather than ask for one the head can't follow (it tells you when it does).

The sizes are angles a stage designer would use: a Medium circle swings about 20° each way side to side and 12° up and down; the sweep about 50° each way. A small shape can go round quickly, a big one needs time, so Huge with Very fast will be slowed on most heads. Heads with a smaller pan or tilt range (set on the Stage page) get bigger numbers so they sweep the same angles. Fine-tune the shape sets exact QLC+ numbers instead, and says what angle they come to; a section with its own width there keeps it, so set it back to Auto to use the sizes above.

Heads that face the back

A head mounted the other way round needs its home tilted the other way to point at the audience. In the Stage step, mark it as facing back and set Where it faces; the preview's view from above shows where each head really points.

Chasers and effects

Chasers are your own sequences of steps: some lights in some colors for a moment, then the next. Build them as a table of steps, or import them from your QLC+ show file. Each chaser starts a set time into its section, runs once, loops or bounces. A chaser belongs to the sections that use it; one used in two sections can be shared or copied.

To keep a section dark until its chaser lights it, open the section's Lights step, and under Individual lights click All lights, then switch on Off for this section. The lights then stay dark, out of the pattern and effects, and only the chaser's steps light them. (Individual lights shows in Full mode.)

Effects sit on top of the look: lightning, a blinder hit, a pulse on every beat, sparkle, strobe hits. Each can be aimed at chosen lights.

To strobe one light in a color, say the stage-right front light in red: turn on Strobe hits, click its lights chip and choose just that light, then pick the color and the speed (1, 2 or 4 flashes per beat, at the section's tempo) under the effects. Switch on Only the strobe on these lights to take that light's own look away, so it shows nothing but the red flashes; leave it off to flash on top of the look. Or choose The look's colors: each light then flashes the color its look gives it (from the mood, the Colors card or its own setting), following the look's color changes, with its steady look dark underneath. (The lights chip, color and switch show in Full mode.)

Extra channels (values from QLC+)

Many lights have channels Lightsmith doesn't set itself: a moving head's bee-eye effect and LED ring, a panel's built-in programs, a second set of LEDs. The preview can't show what these do, so work out the values in QLC+: open the Simple Desk with the real lights on and try the channel until you like it. Then, in Full mode, enter the numbers under Extra channels (values from QLC+) at the bottom of the section's Lights step. Lightsmith keeps them with the section, so they survive every re-export; an edit made by hand to a scene Lightsmith exported is replaced the next time you export that song. Each kind of light in your rig (by model and mode) gets its own panel listing its extra channels, taken from its fixture definition.

  • The chips at the top choose which of those lights your changes go to. All are chosen to start with; untick one to give it different values. A channel marked differs has different values on the chosen lights.
  • Each channel has a slider and a number from 0 to 255. Where the definition names its ranges (say "Rotate clockwise slow to fast"), pick one from the dropdown to jump to the middle of that range; the dropdown always shows which range the value is in.
  • Runs of color channels, such as LED 1 to LED 7 on a ring, sit in small blocks under Color channels, each with a color picker. The copy button gives every block like it the same values.
  • The channels you set go into their own scene in the section, with no fade, so an effect number jumps straight to the one you chose. QLC+ holds these channels at their last value, so once a channel is set in any section of the set, every section that leaves it unset (the × button) puts it back to rest: the range the definition calls "No function" or "Off", or 0. That way the bee-eye or a ring effect stops when the section changes.

Maintenance channels such as a reset, and channels the definition says do nothing, aren't offered. The preview doesn't show extra channels, so check them on the real lights.

Previewing

The stage preview shows the selected section as QLC+ will play it, and loops it so a chaser that starts late comes round again (switch Loop off to play it once). Switch between the audience view and the view from above, open it full screen, or pop it out into its own window. Pressing Play on the timeline plays the whole song in order, with the recording if there is one. While it plays, the preview and the highlighted block follow the playhead and a Playing label in the preview's bottom corner names the section, but the settings below stay on the section you picked, so you can keep working while you listen; when you stop, the section the song stopped in is selected. Loop is ignored during playback, so each section hands straight over to the next.

In full screen, the song's transport floats at the foot of the screen (faint until you point at it): play, pause, back to start, previous and next section, jump to a bar, and the time, section and tempo. Space plays and pauses there too, and Esc comes back.

Cueing the show on stage

On stage, MIDI messages from your gear move the lights on: a drum pad, a footswitch, a scene switch on a guitar modeler. The Show control step sets this up. First choose how sections are cued:

Step through each songA trigger per section
Each song is a cue list. Any cue moves it on to its next section, in order. Best when the cues come from what the band already does, such as a guitarist's scene switches, which repeat through a song (Clean for every verse). Each section has its own trigger that starts it directly, from anywhere in the song. Best with a pad or button per section, or someone at the laptop.

Step through each song

  • Cues: list every message that marks a section change, using Learn and pressing the switch. Leave out switches that don't mark a change (a boost you stomp mid-verse would move the lights on too).
  • A section's own cue: something particular can start a section, such as the drum pad that fires its sample. Set it in the section's Timing card. That message joins the song's cues, so choose one nobody presses at other times.
  • Linked sections move on by themselves and need no cue.
  • Next section and Previous section fix a missed or doubled cue.

Every cue moves the song on by one. If two players cue the same change at the same moment, the lights move on twice: give each change to one player, and keep Previous section within reach.

QLC+ 5 and QLC+ 4

QLC+ 5 answers every message in a cue list's list of cues. QLC+ 4 answers only one message per control, so with QLC+ 4 every cue must arrive as the same message. Two ways to get there:

  • Have each device send one shared message as well as its own: many modelers can send several messages per switch.
  • Let a MIDI interface translate them: interfaces with a mapper (for example CME's H4MIDI WC) can turn several messages into one.

Then make that shared message your only cue. Lightsmith warns you on export when a QLC+ 4 file has more than one.

Between songs, and when something goes wrong

Next song and Previous song move through the set in order, and a song can have its own message that jumps straight to it. Safe look stops everything and brings up a steady wash with the heads at home; after it, the next cue carries on from where the song was. It's also the look to use between songs, while you talk. Under The safe look on the Show control page you choose its color, its brightness (very dim to bright) and whether the moving heads stay lit; the preview shows the result. Left alone, it's a medium warm-white wash. A common routine: end each song on a Blackout section timed to the last hit, then press Safe look after the applause. Blackout switches QLC+'s blackout on and off.

The cheat sheet at the bottom of the Show control step lists what every switch does, song by song. Print it and tape it to the floor.

Devices and QLC+ inputs

Each device needs its own input in QLC+ (Inputs/Outputs): a USB MIDI device appears by name, and a MIDI interface usually shows each of its ports separately. In Show control, set which QLC+ universe each device is patched to. Learn adds a device by itself the first time it hears one.

What you get in QLC+

  • Functions for every section, in a folder per song under Lightsmith. Exporting again replaces them; your own functions are left alone.
  • A Set frame on the Virtual Console with a page per song. Each page holds the song's cue list (step cueing) or a button per section (a trigger per section), plus Safe look and Blackout.
  • Only the page that's showing listens to your gear, so the same switch can mean different things in different songs.

Open the file in QLC+, check its inputs and outputs, and switch to Operate mode. Before the first gig, walk through every cue once with the band at their gear.

Saving your work

Lightsmith keeps your work in the browser as you go, with undo and redo. Save project downloads everything (songs, stage, show control and chasers) as one .lightsmith file: keep it as a backup, or open it on another computer. Recordings aren't inside it; load them again there.

New project, under Save project in the sidebar (a + when the sidebar is folded), clears everything for a new show. It asks first and offers Save project file right in that message, so you can keep a copy before anything goes. Undo can't bring a cleared project back.
